NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Concept Draft

Make every square meter count.

A decision and automation system that helps smallholder farmers choose commodities, manage water, and use inputs more efficiently, based on NASA data, soil conditions, local market data, and IoT sensors.

Primary focus

Not expanding land, and not fixing the entire supply chain. FieldShift optimizes the decisions a farmer can still control: what to plant, when to plant it, how much water/input to use, and when to act.

Crop selection Crop rotation Net margin / ha Water efficiency Explainable recommendation IoT action loop
North-star metric
Risk-adjusted net margin per hectare

Not simply chasing the highest yield. The system looks for the best net income within acceptable water, climate, soil, and market risk limits.

01 · Problem definition

The problem isn't just small landholdings — it's the per-hectare decisions that are still not optimized.

Smallholder farmers have very little room for error, yet lack an integrated system that turns climate, soil, crop, market, and real-time field data into commodity choices and cultivation actions that measurably maximize net income per hectare. Pitch-ready English: "Smallholder farmers with limited land lack an integrated, field-specific system that converts Earth observation, soil, market, farmer, and real-time sensor data into crop and resource-management decisions that maximize risk-adjusted net income per hectare."
An important course correction: the right target is not "maximum yield". A crop with a high tonnage yield does not necessarily deliver the highest margin once seed, fertilizer, water, labor, crop-failure risk, and selling price are all accounted for.
Symptom at farmer level Decision problem Impact on small landholdings What the system can control
Choosing crops out of habit Soil suitability, water, climate, calendar, cost, and price are not evaluated together. One wrong decision can wipe out most of a season's income. Compare several commodity/rotation scenarios transparently.
Watering on a fixed schedule Does not follow actual moisture levels, crop stage, or the chance of rain. Water, pump energy, and labor are wasted. Moisture sensors + irrigation rules + pump/valve control.
Data exists but is siloed Satellite imagery, weather, soil, cost, and price never become one unified "field state". Farmers receive information, but not an operational decision. Data integration and per-field recommendations.
Recommendations stop at information No tool exists to execute and monitor the action. Low adoption and results that are hard to evaluate. IoT, alerts, action logs, and end-of-season feedback.

In scope

  • Selecting feasible commodities and rotations.
  • Calculating margin, water needs, and risk.
  • Planting and irrigation schedules, plus action alerts.
  • Monitoring vegetation health and water use.
  • Mapping assistance needs based on aggregated data.

Out of scope

  • Land redistribution or expanding land ownership.
  • Law enforcement or subsidy-leakage policing.
  • Eliminating middlemen or regulating market prices.
  • Replacing agronomists, extension workers, or farmer decisions.
  • Guaranteeing profit or harvest yield.
02 · Proposed solution

FieldShift: from data → decision → action → learning.

The platform combines Earth observation data, field profile, local economics, and field sensors. A scenario engine computes the options; an LLM explains the results in plain language; IoT helps carry out the action.

1. Farmer & FieldField polygon, previous crops, irrigation, capital, risk preference.
2. NASA & Local DataVegetation, rainfall, historical climate, soil, calendar, cost, and local prices.
3. IoT Ground TruthSoil moisture, microclimate, water flow, pump status.
4. Scenario EngineTests agronomic feasibility and rotation for each commodity.
5. Margin & RiskComputes net income, water needs, and risk penalties.
6. LLM ExplainabilityExplains reasons, trade-offs, and next steps; not the primary scoring driver.
7. Action LoopAlerts, irrigation control, outcome monitoring, and recommendation updates.
Don't feed raw data straight to an LLM to decide the crop. Use rules/MCDA or a measurable model for agronomic and economic scoring. The LLM is used as a conversational and explanatory layer so results stay consistent, auditable, and independent of generative guesswork.
Expected Net Margin / ha = (Predicted Yield × Farm-gate Price) − Total Variable Cost Then adjusted for drought risk, price volatility, data uncertainty, soil impact, and the farmer's capital constraints.
Module Function Output for the farmer
Field Profiler Builds a single field profile from satellite, soil, weather, IoT, and farmer input. Water status, vegetation condition, soil health, planting calendar, and data quality.
Crop & Rotation Engine Filters out unsuitable commodities, then scores the feasible options. Top 3 options with reasoning, trade-offs, and confidence.
Economic Engine Calculates income, cost, price sensitivity, and capital needs. Net margin range per hectare and the break-even price point.
Action Planner Turns the recommendation into an operational schedule. Planting window, irrigation targets, milestones, and risk alerts.
LLM Assistant Translates engine output and answers questions grounded in already-computed data. Plain-language explanation: "why", "what's the risk", and "what to do next".
Learning Loop Compares predictions with actual water use, growth, yield, and margin. Better-calibrated recommendations for the next season.
03 · Data design

NASA provides broad context; IoT provides ground truth on the plot.

Satellite data resolution is not always enough for small fields. That's why NASA data is used for trends and context, while field sensors provide local validation and trigger action. The posture is NASA-first: NASA sources anchor the data layer, and any non-NASA source is labeled explicitly.

Earth observation
NASA HLS 30 m vegetation index (NDVI/NDWI), the primary vegetation-trend signal, and land-condition change. Sentinel-2 L2A 10 m detail layer that sharpens HLS where higher resolution is available. GPM IMERG rainfall and regional dry-spell detection. SMAP regional soil-moisture context. NASA POWER daily climate and meteorology baseline — this replaces the Open-Meteo-style source used in the earlier draft. GEOGLAM Crop Monitor regional crop-condition outlook.
Local agronomy
pH, SOC, soil texture, drainage, irrigation type, previous crops, local calendar, and agronomist recommendations. SoilGrids — non-NASA ISRIC's global soil-property grids, used to fill soil-attribute gaps and explicitly labeled as a non-NASA source.
Farm economics
farm-gate prices, seed, fertilizer, labor, water/energy, and post-harvest costs, capital, and available buyers.
IoT telemetry
soil moisture per depth, air temperature/humidity, water flow, pump/valve status, and an optional rain gauge.
Farmer constraints
income target, risk tolerance, capital, labor, crop preference, seed access, and limits on switching commodities.
Design implication: HLS helps read vegetation patterns at a 30-meter scale, while IMERG and most SMAP products are better suited as broader-area context. The system must never present regional data as a "direct measurement" of a single plot; IoT and farmer observations close that gap.

05 · IoT solution

Not just giving advice — helping to carry it out.

Minimum viable field kit

💧
Soil-moisture sensorsTwo depths to read the root zone, not just the surface.
🌡️
Air temperature & humidityMeasures the local microclimate and heat stress.
🚰
Water-flow meterMeasures actual volume and detects abnormal flow.
⚙️
Pump relay / solenoid valveRuns irrigation with manual override and a time limit.
📡
GatewayLoRa, GSM, or Wi-Fi depending on village conditions; still stores data when the connection drops.
pH, SOC, and soil texture only need periodic measurement for the MVP; they don't need to be forced into real-time sensors.

Closed-loop irrigation

SenseRead moisture, temperature, rainfall, and flow.
DecideCompare against crop-stage thresholds and the forecast.
ActTurn on the pump/valve until target or safety limit is reached.
VerifyConfirm flow occurred and moisture responded.

Simple rule example

IF soil_moisture < crop_threshold
AND rain_probability_next_6h is low
AND daily_water_limit not exceeded
THEN open valve for N minutes
ELSE send alert / wait

Safety guardrails

Manual override Max runtime No-flow alert Sensor-quality flag Offline local rule
06 · Side branch

Government/NGO support helps close the adoption gap — it does not take over the decision.

This feature is supportive in nature. Individual data is shared only with consent and should preferably be aggregated/anonymized.

Core farmer product

Field-level decision & action

  • Irrigation and input plan.
  • IoT monitoring and alerts.
  • Explainable recommendations.

Sensor-as-a-service

Extension workers validate recommendations, prioritize visits, and log field outcomes.

07 · MVP plan

Build small, but closed-loop.

It's more convincing to demo one decision all the way through to an IoT action than to display lots of data with no proof of use.

Success metrics

Margin / haProjected vs. actual, per season.
Water / haVolume of water per phase and per outcome.
Prediction errorYield, rainfall, and soil-moisture response.
AdoptionRecommendations understood and actually followed.
Data confidenceShare of decisions backed by adequate-quality input.
Hypotheses that must be validated before the final pitch: farmers are willing to switch commodities, have access to seed and buyers, are willing to use sensors, and have enough cost/price data to calculate margin. Run short interviews with farmers and extension workers.
