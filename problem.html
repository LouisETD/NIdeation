NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Concept Draft

Make every square meter count.

A decision-support prototype that helps smallholder farmers protect seasonal income by comparing feasible crop plans against water, capital, climate, and market assumptions.

Primary focus

Limited land leaves little room for a bad season. FieldShift helps farmers compare what to plant next and how water, capital, yield, and price assumptions change the expected margin.

Crop selection Crop rotation Net margin / ha Water efficiency Explainable recommendation IoT ground truth Stress Lab
North-star metric
Risk-adjusted net margin per hectare

Not simply chasing the highest yield. The system looks for the best net income within acceptable water, climate, soil, and market risk limits.

01 · Problem definition

Smallholder farmers have little room for error when a season can erase already-thin income.

Smallholder farmers with limited land can struggle to earn a stable livelihood when water, weather, input costs, and crop prices shift. They lack a clear way to compare feasible planting plans and see how those changes affect income per hectare. Pitch-ready English: "Smallholder farmers with limited land lack a clear way to combine Earth observation, local soil and market context, farmer constraints, and field measurements to compare feasible crop plans by risk-adjusted net margin per hectare."
An important course correction: the right target is not "maximum yield". A crop with a high tonnage yield does not necessarily deliver the highest margin once seed, fertilizer, water, labor, crop-failure risk, and selling price are all accounted for.
Symptom at farmer level Decision problem Impact on small landholdings What the system provides
Choosing crops out of habit Soil suitability, water, climate, calendar, cost, and price are not evaluated together. One wrong decision can wipe out most of a season's income. Compare several commodity/rotation scenarios transparently.
Watering on a fixed schedule Does not follow actual moisture levels, crop stage, or the chance of rain. Water, pump energy, and labor are wasted. Read soil moisture, microclimate, flow, and pump status as field evidence.
Data exists but is siloed Satellite imagery, weather, soil, cost, and price never become one unified "field state". Farmers receive information, but not an operational decision. Data integration and per-field recommendations.
Crop choices are hard to compare Yield alone hides costs, water limits, capital needs, and downside exposure. Farmers may take on a plan whose downside they cannot absorb. Show feasibility, assumptions, margin, and a transparent downside case.

In scope

  • Selecting feasible commodities and rotations.
  • Calculating margin, water needs, and risk.
  • Testing one next-season crop change under explicit assumptions.
  • Read-only field monitoring to ground-truth model inputs.
  • Mapping assistance needs based on aggregated data.

Out of scope

  • Land redistribution or expanding land ownership.
  • Law enforcement or subsidy-leakage policing.
  • Eliminating middlemen or regulating market prices.
  • Replacing agronomists, extension workers, or farmer decisions.
  • Guaranteeing profit or harvest yield.
02 · Proposed solution

FieldShift: from field evidence → feasible plan → farmer review.

A scenario engine checks soil, calendar, water, and capital, then ranks feasible options by margin after an explicit downside reserve. The farmer reviews the recommendation; sensors only record field conditions.

1. Farmer & FieldField polygon, previous crops, irrigation, capital, risk preference.
2. NASA & Local DataVegetation, rainfall, historical climate, soil, calendar, cost, and local prices.
3. IoT Ground TruthSoil moisture, microclimate, water flow, pump status.
4. Scenario EngineTests agronomic feasibility and rotation for each commodity.
5. Margin & RiskComputes net income, water needs, and risk penalties.
6. Explainable resultShows gates, assumptions, margin, downside case, and ranking reason.
7. Farmer reviewCompare one crop change for the next season; no pump or valve commands.
The prototype uses deterministic rules for feasibility and ranking. A stress case is a transparent assumption, not a probability or forecast. Verify local agronomy, prices, and sensor calibration before using the result for a real planting decision.
Expected Net Margin / ha = (Predicted Yield × Farm-gate Price) − Total Variable Cost Scenario ranking uses a transparent downside reserve. The separate 3D Stress Lab shows plant stress and qualitative market context; it does not calculate revenue or margin.
Module Function Output for the farmer
Field Profiler Builds a single field profile from satellite, soil, weather, IoT, and farmer input. Water status, vegetation condition, soil health, planting calendar, and data quality.
Crop & Rotation Engine Filters out unsuitable commodities, then scores the feasible options. Top 3 options with reasoning, trade-offs, and confidence.
Economic Engine Calculates income, cost, price sensitivity, and capital needs. Net margin range per hectare and the break-even price point.
Stress Lab Replays 12 illustrative crop weeks in 3D; tests rainfall, irrigation, heat, one crop change, and separate price/harvest assumptions. Plant-condition and stress indices, water-reserve status, and qualitative profitability context; no money formula.
Field Ground Truth Records probe readings, air conditions, flow, pump contact, and surface photos. Timestamped observations with separate source and quality labels.
Farmer review Checks ranked plans against local knowledge and available seed or buyers. A clear next-season comparison to discuss with an agronomist.
03 · Data design

NASA provides broad context; IoT provides ground truth on the plot.

Satellite data resolution is not always enough for small fields. That's why NASA data is used for trends and context, while field sensors provide local measurements for comparison. The posture is NASA-first: NASA sources anchor the data layer, and any non-NASA source is labeled explicitly.

Earth observation
NASA HLS 30 m vegetation index (NDVI/NDWI), the primary vegetation-trend signal, and land-condition change. Sentinel-2 L2A 10 m detail layer that sharpens HLS where higher resolution is available. GPM IMERG rainfall and regional dry-spell detection. SMAP regional soil-moisture context. NASA POWER daily climate and meteorology baseline — this replaces the Open-Meteo-style source used in the earlier draft. GEOGLAM Crop Monitor regional crop-condition outlook.
Local agronomy
pH, SOC, soil texture, drainage, irrigation type, previous crops, local calendar, and agronomist recommendations. SoilGrids — non-NASA ISRIC's global soil-property grids, used to fill soil-attribute gaps and explicitly labeled as a non-NASA source.
Farm economics
farm-gate prices, seed, fertilizer, labor, water/energy, and post-harvest costs, capital, and available buyers.
IoT telemetry
root-zone soil moisture from a probe; air temperature/humidity; pipe flow; isolated, read-only pump status; and surface photos.
Farmer constraints
income target, risk tolerance, capital, labor, crop preference, seed access, and limits on switching commodities.
Design implication: HLS helps read vegetation patterns at a 30-meter scale, while IMERG and most SMAP products are better suited as broader-area context. The system must never present regional data as a "direct measurement" of a single plot. A probe gives the soil-moisture value; a camera gives separate visual evidence.

05 · IoT ground truth

A small field kit observes conditions; it does not control irrigation.

One pilot node records soil moisture, microclimate, flow, pump status, and surface imagery. It sends timestamped evidence to the app when connected.

One pilot kit

💧
Root-zone moisture probeNumeric reading from the soil; calibrate against local soil before claiming accuracy.
📷
Surface cameraVisual evidence only; it does not measure soil moisture.
🌡️
Air temperature & humidityLocal microclimate reading near the plot.
🚰
Inline flow meterMeasures water moving through the pipe.
🔌
Isolated pump-status inputReads a control contact; no relay output or pump command.

Two delivery modes

RealtimeSensor sample every 60 seconds; one surface photo every 5 minutes.
BatchSensor sample every 30 minutes; two surface photos per day, uploaded when connected.
Each reading carries its own source, observation time, upload time, and quality state. Missing or stale data must not look current.
06 · Side branch

Government/NGO support helps close the adoption gap — it does not take over the decision.

This feature is supportive in nature. Individual data is shared only with consent and should preferably be aggregated/anonymized.

Core farmer product

Field-level decision support

  • Commodity/rotation choice and margin.
  • Irrigation and input plan.
  • IoT monitoring and alerts.
  • Explainable recommendations.

Optional support layer

Needs & intervention map

  • Locations that need soil testing or loaned sensors.
  • Seed, training, irrigation, or capital needs based on aggregation.
  • List of villages with buyer/off-taker gaps.
  • Monitoring aid usage and pilot outcomes.
🧪

Soil-testing support

Mobile lab, soil-test vouchers, and standardized results to strengthen model inputs.

🛰️

Sensor-as-a-service

Loan or co-financing programs to reduce the per-farmer cost of hardware.

🧑‍🌾

Extension dashboard

Extension workers validate recommendations, prioritize visits, and log field outcomes.

Success metrics

Margin / haProjected vs. actual, per season.
Water / haVolume of water per phase and per outcome.
Prediction errorYield, rainfall, and soil-moisture response.
AdoptionRecommendations understood and actually followed.
Data confidenceShare of decisions backed by adequate-quality input.
Hypotheses that must be validated before the final pitch: farmers are willing to switch commodities, have access to seed and buyers, are willing to use sensors, and have enough cost/price data to calculate margin. Run short interviews with farmers and extension workers.

FieldShift helps smallholder farmers protect seasonal income by combining NASA context, local soil and market data, and read-only field measurements to compare feasible crop plans by risk-adjusted margin.
