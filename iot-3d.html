Why sensor-as-a-service is plausible here: at pilot volume the sensing node alone runs about $80.00 / Rp 1,264,000, and a shared field kit with one actuation point adds roughly $16.20 / Rp 255,960 more — together $96.20 / Rp 1,519,960. That is more than many smallholder farmers can justify buying outright against a single season's margin on a small plot. It is well within reach, though, for an NGO, cooperative, or local government agency financing a 100–300 unit pilot batch (about $16,000 for 200 nodes) and leasing or co-financing devices per farmer per season — the same shared-equipment model already used for tractors and pumps in rural Indonesia, spreading one fixed hardware cost across many growing seasons instead of asking one farmer to absorb it up front.

A field valve, relay driver, and flow meter are priced separately below — one actuation point can typically serve several sensing nodes on the same plot, so it isn't multiplied per node.

Power budget

Sized to run for years between battery swaps.

The arithmetic below is deliberately spelled out so it can be checked by hand: current draw × duration × events per day, converted to mAh/day, against a de-rated battery capacity and the solar income that recharges it.

Autonomy, worked through:
Pack capacity: 2 × 3,400 mAh 18650 cells in parallel = 6,800 mAh nominal. (Genuine 18650 cells top out near 3,500 mAh — listings advertising “5,000 mAh 18650” are re-wrapped and should not be designed against.)
Usable capacity after de-rating: 6,800 × 0.80 (depth-of-discharge ceiling, protects cycle life) × 0.90 (heat/humidity capacity loss) = 4,896 mAh.
Autonomy with zero solar input: 4,896 ÷ 16.59 ≈ 295 days on paper.

What we actually claim: ≥60 days. The paper figure is not the honest number. Past roughly two months the binding constraints stop being energy and become calendar ageing, connector corrosion, and probe drift. We size the pack for a worst-case monsoon overcast plus panel soiling and a flat-battery cold start — not to advertise a year of darkness. Energy is not the limiting factor in this design; serviceability is.

Solar income, de-rated for the wet season: 2 W panel × 1.2 effective peak-sun-hours/day (monsoon overcast and a soiled panel, versus ≈4.5 on a clear tropical day) × 75% charge-path efficiency = 1.8 Wh/day ÷ 3.7 V ≈ 486 mAh/day.
Recharge margin in the worst month: 486 ÷ 16.59 ≈ 29× the daily load. The panel is oversized on purpose — it has to recover the pack after a multi-week overcast run, not merely balance a day.

Installation

Where each part sits in the field.

Cross-section is schematic, not to scale — depths and heights are called out explicitly instead.

  • Probe depths: 15 cm sits in the fast-draining feeder-root zone the closed-loop rule reacts to first; 40 cm distinguishes a genuinely dry root zone from a surface that has simply dried between waterings.
  • Enclosure height: mounted clear of mature canopy for the deployed crop (canopy-dependent) so the solar panel keeps sun exposure and the antenna keeps radio line-of-sight to the gateway.
  • Stake orientation: driven vertically, roughly 35 cm into the soil, anchoring the enclosure and antenna above casual contact from livestock or field tools.
  • Cable strain relief: the sensor cable exits through a single IP68 M12 gland at the enclosure base, with a service loop before it enters the soil, so tension or a farmer accidentally pulling the cable doesn't stress the sealed entry point.
  • Single penetration: the gland is the only place water can track into the enclosure — everything else is a moulded, gasketed seam.
Safety guardrails

The valve fails closed, always.

Every guardrail below resolves to the same fail-safe state: irrigation stops and the valve returns to closed.

TriggerSystem responseFail-safe state
Manual override
Farmer flips the physical switch or sends an override command		 Immediately halts any automatic actuation and surfaces a manual-mode banner in the app Valve closed until the farmer re-enables auto mode
Max runtime exceeded
Valve has been open longer than the configured cap (e.g. 20 min)		 Controller force-closes the valve and raises a maintenance alert, regardless of the moisture reading Valve closed
No-flow alert
Relay commands "open" but the Hall-effect flow meter reports no pulses for >30 s		 Assumes a stuck valve, blocked line, or dry supply; aborts the cycle and flags for inspection Valve commanded closed, irrigation suspended until acknowledged
Sensor-quality flag
A probe reading is out of physically plausible range, the two probes disagree beyond threshold, or a reading is stale		 Decision engine discards the suspect input, falls back to the last known-good reading, and raises a maintenance flag No new irrigation triggered off a flagged sensor — valve stays/returns closed
Offline local rule
LoRa uplink/downlink lost beyond N cycles		 Node reverts to running the closed-loop rule entirely locally, using only its own probes and a conservative pre-agreed daily water cap If local data is also uncertain, default is no action / valve closed
Closed-loop rule

Sense, decide, act, verify.

The same four-step loop the guardrails above are built to interrupt safely at any point.

IF soil_moisture < crop_threshold
AND rain_probability_next_6h is low
AND daily_water_limit not exceeded
THEN open valve for N minutes
ELSE send alert / wait
1. SenseRead 15 cm & 40 cm soil moisture, air temp/RH, and battery state.
2. DecideCompare against crop threshold, rain forecast, and the daily water cap.
3. ActOpen the valve for N minutes, or send an alert and wait.
4. VerifyConfirm real flow via the Hall-effect meter before marking the cycle complete.
